# Who organises flea markets, and what actually hurts them

Web research, 2026-09-16. Switzerland and Germany. Everything below is from organisers' own pages, their rules ("Marktordnung"), the tools they buy, and press. No interviews yet — that is the obvious next step.

## 1. The segments

| Segment | Markets / year | Who does the work | Tech level | Tools already in use | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Commercial multi-market operator (DE)** | 50–1,500 | A company with staff (allocation, cash, stewards, electricians) | High; own booking systems | Own online shop with login and seat map; SMS to vendors; hotline | Melan: 1,500 markets/yr, 4M visitors, 24/7 online booking ([melan.de](https://www.melan.de/fuer-alle/ueber-melan/)); Hochberg, nmv-Marktagentur (own ticket shops); Mauerpark: in-person reservation only, Sundays 10–13, SMS for storm closures ([mv-perske.de FAQ](https://www.mv-perske.de/faq.html)) |
| **Commercial single weekly market (CH)** | 40–52 (one venue) | Owner-operator, a few helpers | Medium; a website with a booking form | Own site, Instagram, phone | Kanzlei Zürich: online booking Thursday 12:00, "sold out in 15–20 minutes", CHF 20–90 ([conviva-plus.ch](https://www.conviva-plus.ch/?page=1936)); Rosenhof: walk-in |
| **Municipality / canton** | 20–30 (one season) | Civil servants in a markets office | High; buy market software | ShoWare ticketing with Twint/Visa (Basel); lottery portal (Zürich) | Bürkliplatz: city-run, lottery Tue 07:00–Wed 10:00, CHF 42 per 3 m day pitch ([stadt-zuerich.ch](https://www.stadt-zuerich.ch/de/stadtleben/veranstaltungen-und-bewilligungen/gewerbe-und-maerkte/maerkte/flohmaerkte.html)); Petersplatz Basel: CHF 20 + 10/m, book Tue–Thu online ([bs.ch](https://www.bs.ch/verwaltung/praesidialdepartement/aemter-und-bereiche/aussenbeziehungen-und-standortmarketing/messen-und-maerkte/basler-maerkte/flohmarkt-petersplatz)) |

Two things stand out. The professionals already own booking software and do not need ours. Everyone else runs one to eight events a year, mostly on a club website plus e-mail plus Instagram, and books stalls through Ticketpark, Eventfrog or a form.

## 2. The five recurring pains, ranked

**1. Filling the stalls — or rationing them.** This is what permits and economics both hinge on. Zürich only grants a market licence to a club that can show "at least five different vendors with 45 running metres who take part regularly" ([stadt-zuerich.ch](https://www.stadt-zuerich.ch/de/stadtleben/veranstaltungen-und-bewilligungen/gewerbe-und-maerkte/maerkte/flohmaerkte.html)). Guides tell new organisers to plan six months of vendor recruitment ([meinverein.de](https://www.meinverein.de/blog/vereinsbuchhaltung-finanzierung/flohmarkt-fuer-mehr-geld-in-der-vereinskasse/)). The mirror image in cities is too much demand: Kanzlei sells out in 15 minutes, Bürkliplatz runs a lottery. Either way, the stall side, not the visitor side, is what organisers manage day to day.

**2. Deciding and announcing a weather cancellation.** Organisers call it "a very difficult decision" and describe the blame from both sides ([flohmarkt-altefeld.de](https://flohmarkt-altefeld.de/wetter-laune-und-flohmarkt/)). The broadcast is a patchwork: Offenbach may cancel "on Saturday morning on site" ([flohmarkt-offenbach.com](https://www.flohmarkt-offenbach.com/)); Bad Tölz decides "the day before around 14:00, at the latest 07:00 on the day" and publishes on a phone hotline ([toelzer-flohmarkt.de](https://toelzer-flohmarkt.de/)); Mauerpark texts every vendor by SMS ([mv-perske.de](https://www.mv-perske.de/faq.html)); Bürkliplatz posts on "the GZ website, Instagram and Facebook"; a Steyr organiser runs an admin-only WhatsApp broadcast group precisely for this ([mega-flohmarkt.info](https://www.mega-flohmarkt.info/anmeldung-info-whatsapp-gruppe/)). In Switzerland the traditional answer was the 1600 phone line, which Swisscom shut down at the end of 2022; clubs "switched to app solutions" ([luzernerzeitung.ch](https://www.luzernerzeitung.ch/leben/telefonauskunft-findet-die-schulreise-statt-wieso-es-auf-diese-frage-ueber-kurznummern-bald-keine-infos-mehr-gibt-ld.2308541)). Refund rules are where vendor anger lands: some refund minus fees ([flohmaxx.de](https://flohmaxx.de/flohmarktordnung/)), many refuse any refund for force majeure ([asikan.org](https://asikan.org/marktordnung.html)).

**3. Registration admin: allocation, payment, the same questions.** The two paid tools sell against one enemy: "Endlich kein Excel-Chaos mehr", "früher war das reine Handarbeit" ([flohmarkt-verwaltung.de](https://www.flohmarkt-verwaltung.de/)), and Mercalix promises to end "email chaos" ([mercalixfusion.com](https://mercalixfusion.com/)). The Mauerpark FAQ is a list of the questions vendors ask every week: same pitch as last time? set-up time? can I drive in? how do I cancel? ([mv-perske.de](https://www.mv-perske.de/faq.html)). St. Mangen's site says bluntly that e-mail reservations are not accepted — a volunteer refusing to be an inbox.

**4. Permits and rules.** Real but once a year, and well documented by cities themselves (Zürich: CHF 300 per running metre per season plus plans; Bonn, Kiel, the IHK Siegen guide). Not something a directory can take off their plate.

**5. Publicity across many portals.** Germany has at least eight free "Termin eintragen" portals, each with its own login (flohmarkt-termine.org, flohmarktradar, flohmarktkalender.com, wasgehtapp, flohmarktseite.de …); openeventnetwork.de exists only to cure this. Honest reading: organisers mostly do not do it, which is why directory data is stale and why every portal carries a "we do not know the events, ask the organiser" disclaimer ([flohmarkttermine.ch](https://flohmarkttermine.ch/)). It is our pain more than theirs.

## 3. What they pay today

- Municipalities: Mercalix Fusion CHF 130 / 650 / 3,900 a year plus CHF 1,000 setup ([mercalixfusion.com](https://mercalixfusion.com/)).
- Commercial operators: own systems, or FlohmarktPro on a per-event fee with a free tier ([flohmarkt-verwaltung.de](https://www.flohmarkt-verwaltung.de/)).
- Parent committees: basarlino at 1 EUR per seller plus 1% of turnover, often passed on to sellers; UnserBasar from 10 EUR ([basarlino.de](https://basarlino.de/)).
- Clubs: Ticketpark/Eventfrog per-ticket fees on stall bookings; permit fees (Zürich CHF 42 per day pitch; a season licence at CHF 300/m).
- Directories: essentially nothing. flohmarktkompass's 5 EUR/month upsell is the ceiling anyone has tried (already in `PRODUCT.md`).

Volunteer segments pay for the permit and, sometimes, a per-stall ticketing fee. They do not pay for software.

## 4. What would bring them back weekly — honestly

Only weekly commercial markets have a weekly job, and they already have their own booking systems and Instagram. Everyone else has one to eight events a year; their return frequency will equal their event frequency, and no feature changes that. Do not design for weekly logins.

What does bring a volunteer back at the moments that matter:

- Something to do the week before the market: confirm the date, see how many people looked at the page, push a "still on" or "cancelled" status.
- A reason to point their own site at ours. If Fynda is where "findet der Flohmi statt?" gets answered, they link to it — and come back to set it.
- Proof of reach, once: "412 people viewed your market this month, 38 saved it, 19 subscribed to the digest for your town." That is the sentence that makes the next confirmation e-mail get opened.

## 5. The single cheapest thing that would actually be used

Not a login. No segment above wants one more account on a directory; the eight German portals with organiser logins prove it by their stale data.

The cheapest useful thing is a **signed link in an e-mail, per market, no account**: it opens a page with three buttons — *Next date is correct* / *Cancelled today (weather)* / *Something changed*. Pressing one updates the Fynda page (and its `eventStatus` for Google), the "Bestätigt am" line, the Friday digest, and the ICS feed within a minute. Send it automatically ten days before each date, and again at 06:00 on a rainy market morning.

Why this and not stall booking: booking is solved for the people who need it (Ticketpark, Eventfrog, basarlino, own shops), and building a rival is months of work against free tools. Cancellation broadcast is unsolved — the 1600 line is gone and the replacement is "check our website, Instagram and Facebook". A public status page that says *Findet statt / Abgesagt* with a timestamp, which the organiser can link from a club site or WhatsApp group, replaces a phone tree and costs us a page template.

Second-cheapest, and complementary: a **vendor waiting-list address** on the market page ("want a stall? leave your e-mail") forwarded to the organiser. It attacks pain 1 for small clubs without any software, and it is the one thing a directory can give that a booking tool cannot: reach.

## Gaps to close before building

- No organiser has been asked directly. Five twenty-minute calls (a Quartierverein, a GZ, a parent committee, Kanzlei, one canton office) would confirm or kill the ranking above. Delfim cannot run these in German; they need a German speaker or an English-speaking Zürich contact.
- No Facebook-group or Reddit vendor complaints surfaced in web search; that evidence lives inside closed groups.
- Shopping-centre and hall-owner segments are thin here; one operator (Melan) covers most of it in Germany.
