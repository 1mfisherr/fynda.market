City page — four structures

Zürich, 14 markets. Deliberately low fidelity: grey blocks, no photographs, no names. The question is which shape, not which shade — pick a structure and I will build it properly. Phone on the left, desktop on the right in each pair.

A. Lead + list

The next market at full size, then the other thirteen compact. Same pattern as the home page you just approved, so the site holds together with one idea. Strong first impression, and the rest stays dense enough to scan.
Weak spot: the lead is only "next", which is not necessarily the best one.

B. Card grid

Every market a card with a picture on top. The most visual option and the least like a directory — closest to how people browse things they might want. Two columns on desktop, one on a phone.
Weak spot: no hierarchy at all, and fourteen equal cards is a long page on a phone. Cards also hide the date unless it is set loud.

C. Grouped by how often it runs

Three groups: every week · every month · rare — once or twice a year. This is the only option that uses something no competitor has, and it answers the real question: can I go any Saturday, or is this the one chance this year? The rare group becomes genuinely urgent.
Weak spot: a heading people have to read to understand, and the weekly group will be the biggest and least exciting.

D. Weekend by weekend

The next four weekends as blocks, each listing what is on. Answers "when shall I go" rather than "what exists", and it makes a plan rather than a directory. Also the shape closest to how the data actually behaves.
Weak spot: the same weekly markets repeat in every block — the exact problem we just spent the day removing.

If you want my pick

A, with C's grouping inside it. A keeps the site consistent — one idea, home and city — and gives the page a face.
